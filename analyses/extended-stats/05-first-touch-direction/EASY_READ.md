# First-Touch Direction — Plain-English Table

When the 9:30 candle eventually gets retested, **which side gets touched first**? This tests whether there's an inherent direction-bias to which extreme the price returns to first.

## Headline table

(Sample = 223 trading days)

| What happens first | Days in sample | % of days |
|---|---|---|
| The high gets touched first | 107 | **47.98%** |
| The low gets touched first | 104 | **46.64%** |
| Both touched in the same 15-min bar | 12 | 5.38% |
| Never touched by end of day | 0 | 0.00% |

**Plain reading:** First-touch is essentially **a coin flip** — 48/47 with a tiny lean to high. There is **no inherent direction bias** to which side gets retested first.

## Why this matters

The previously found "high-side bias" in the cumulative stats (where high gets retested ~58% vs low ~54%) is NOT because the high gets *touched first* more often. It's because after a sweep (both sides), the high tends to come second more often.

In other words: the directional information is in the candle's CLOSE COLOR (see analysis #04), not in the time-direction asymmetry of which side comes first.

## Trade implication

You **cannot predict** which side gets retested first without additional information (like the candle's close color). On any given day, treat first-touch as 50/50.

## How reliable?

223 days. Aggregate ±3pp. The 48/47 split is far too close to 50/50 to suggest any directional edge.

## Glossary

- **First-touch**: the side (high or low) that gets reached first after the candle closes.
- **Same-bar ambiguous**: both extremes were touched within the same 15-minute candle, so we can't say which came first.
