# Conditional on Candle Color — Plain-English Table

Does it matter whether the 9:30 candle closed green (bullish) or red (bearish)? **Yes, dramatically.**

We split the 9:30 candles into green (close > open) and red (close < open) and recompute hit rates separately.

## Headline table

(Sample: 119 green days, 111 red days, 2 doji days excluded)

### After a GREEN 9:30 candle:

| Outcome | By 10:00 | By 10:15 |
|---|---|---|
| Either side touched | 89.92% | 98.32% |
| Both sides touched | 5.04% | 14.29% |
| **High touched (any)** | **73.95%** | **78.99%** |
| Low touched (any) | 21.01% | 33.61% |

### After a RED 9:30 candle:

| Outcome | By 10:00 | By 10:15 |
|---|---|---|
| Either side touched | 90.99% | 95.50% |
| Both sides touched | 7.21% | 18.02% |
| High touched (any) | 24.32% | 36.04% |
| **Low touched (any)** | **73.87%** | **77.48%** |

**Plain reading:** **The candle's color is a near-mirror predictor of which side gets retested.** After a green close, the HIGH gets retested 74% of the time — but the low only 21%. After a red close, the LOW gets retested 74% — but the high only 24%.

This is the strongest directional signal in any of our analyses.

## What this means in practice

- Green 9:30 candle? You can plan a fade trade at the high with about a 3:1 edge.
- Red 9:30 candle? You can plan a fade at the low with the same edge.
- The candle's close direction tells you which side of the range to focus on.

## How reliable?

230 days (split between green and red). Each sub-sample is ~110-120 days. Aggregate ±3-4pp per side. The asymmetry (74% vs 21%) is far too large to be statistical noise.

## Glossary

- **Green candle**: a candle that closed higher than it opened (close > open). Bullish.
- **Red candle**: closed lower than it opened (close < open). Bearish.
- **Doji**: open equals close exactly — neither green nor red. Excluded here.
