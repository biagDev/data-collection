# Range Statistics — Plain-English Table

How big is the typical 9:30 candle compared to a full trading day's range?

## Headline numbers

(Sample = 223 trading days)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Average size of the 9:30-9:45 candle | **109 NQ points** |
| Average size of the full RTH range (9:30-16:00) | **309 NQ points** |
| Average ratio (9:30 candle as % of full day) | **38.7%** |

**Plain reading:** The 9:30 candle alone establishes about **39% of the full day's range** on average. The first 15 minutes of trading sets a very large fraction of the day's price expansion.

## Distribution table

| The 9:30 candle's size relative to the full day | Days in sample | % of days |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 25% of the full day's range | 37 | 16.59% |
| 25-50% (typical) | 143 | **64.13%** |
| 50-75% | 38 | 17.04% |
| 75% or more (the open IS the day's range) | 5 | 2.24% |

**Plain reading:** On about **2/3 of days**, the 9:30 candle is somewhere between a quarter and half of the full day's range. About **2% of days** the opening 15 minutes is so wide that very little additional expansion happens — these are extreme low-volatility days after the open.

## Trade implication

If you're sizing a target based on the 9:30 candle's range:
- Most days, you can expect 1-2x the opening range to be available later.
- About 1 in 6 days you'll get a much bigger move (opening was less than 25% of day).
- About 1 in 50 days the open already used all the range — wait those out.

## How reliable?

223 days, ~11 months. Aggregate ±2pp.

## Glossary

- **9:30 candle range**: high minus low of the 9:30-9:45 ET candle.
- **Full RTH range**: high minus low of the entire regular session (9:30-16:00 ET).
- **NQ point**: 1 point of NQ (Nasdaq-100 e-mini futures) = $20 per contract.
